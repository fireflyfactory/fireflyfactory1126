AUTOVIVISECTION A monk and an actress are sitting at a bar. There is a chimpanzee asleep across from them. The fighter planes are a few miles out. The monk is an old man, wearing a wool garb, drinking a beer. The actress is wearing a very beautiful silk dress and heels. She is having a martini. The chimp is naked, as is his custom, and is still asleep. An empty bottle of gin is next to him. The monk and the actress have their eyes fixed on him, and notice the other one staring. They then notice the auspiciousness of their distinction as two characters, and the coincidence of their meeting as if it were the beginning of a joke, and on that account, the monk decides to attempt to strike up a conversation.
"Is the chimpanzee always here?" he whispers to the woman.
She yelps, startled by the voice, nearly jumping out of her seat. The monk jolts backward and the actress covers her mouth with her hand. Both pause for a second, and then laugh together. The fighter planes are at half a mile and closing, and the monkey stirs slightly.
"I'm sorry, I'm so sorry! You just startled me, is all," the woman says apologetically. "No, I'm sorry for the surprise," the man replies, "I was only curious about the monkey."
She laughs, "I'm worried we'll wake him!"
The monk laughs too.
"And, to answer your question, no I don't know about the ape, this is my first time here." She extends her hand, and the monk grasps it.
"My name is Sophia, by the way."
"I am Joseph. It is a pleasure to meet you, Sophia. A beautiful name for a beautiful woman!"
She blushes and smiles, a real smile and a genuine blush.
The fighter planes are about a quarter mile away and the monkey springs to life. Only he can hear the sky screaming as the jets' wings cut through the air. He knows what they are and he knows what they are here to do: to level his favorite bar in the world. He had been listening to the conversation, and thinking to himself, "what a beautiful moment, the meeting of two kind strangers, and how lucky I am to be a witness, even in this minute form." These two strangers are minutes away from being pulverized by a missile! He must warn them! He stands up on the bar and beats his chest, howling, "Run, run, run! Please, everybody, get far away and find shelter or something, please!" But, he is an ape, and he is drunk, and nobody can understand him. The patrons respond with laughter at what they perceive to be the performance of a temperamental monkey.
Seconds later, the bomb hits the ground: a white flash, an indescribable sound, then the smells and sights of flesh and metal and brick melting, vaporizing, emulsifying in seconds. The bodies of the monk and the actress are instantaneously dismembered and mutilated. A minute passes and the dust clears. From under the bar, the chimpanzee emerges. He is scarred, and missing a few bits. From now on, he will walk with a limp. He surveys the damage, and then he weeps.
